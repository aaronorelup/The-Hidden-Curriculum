### 08 · The terminal: a text window where a shell runs commands as you (50 s, ~115 words)

## 6. Mental model: one worker, many rooms

- **Opening picture (03):** one worker (the model) and a building of rooms with doors labelled Chat, Cowork, Code. The worker can stand behind any door; what changes is what the room holds and which shelves of yours it can reach. The front counter (Chat): talk only, no access to your shelves. The back office (Cowork): you hand over a job and walk away. Your own workshop (Code, Local): works at your bench, with your tools, as you.
- **It transforms:**
  - 05: the doors' labels fade into one door (the merge); the rooms behind it are still different.
  - 06: the back office lifts off and moves across town to the vendor's building (6 October, Pro and Max). A hatch back to one shelf in your house opens only while the desktop app is open; through it pass copies, never the shelf itself.
  - 07: your workshop gets more doors (terminal, desktop, IDE, browser) onto the same bench; a guest room (WSL) and a workshop you reach down a phone line (SSH).
  - 08-09: the workshop's speaking tube: the terminal window, the shell at the other end reading each line, acting with your keys.
  - 10: the rooms you build for it: a detached house (VM, its own foundations: its own operating system) and a flat in a shared building (container, shared foundations: the host's kernel).
  - 11: the vendor's spare workshop working on a copy of your project, fetched from the branch you pushed, not your bench.
  - 12-13: everything that crossed town is a copy, and the landlord keeps a logbook: training, memory, retention, who at work can read it. Different landlords keep different logbooks.
- **Where it breaks (say it plainly, 04 and 06):** (1) it is not always the same worker: some counters get a newer model first (OpenAI, 2026-09-22); (2) rooms move without you moving: on 6 October the back office moved across town for Pro and Max overnight; (3) a thing you carry back from a room is gone from it, but a copy can stay: a deleted chat's memory entries remain, and training already started keeps your data.
