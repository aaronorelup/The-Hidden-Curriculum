# S249 — Mollick, "An opinionated guide to which AI to use to do stuff" (Summer 2026)

**Credit:** Ethan Mollick, "An opinionated guide to which AI to use to do stuff (The Summer 2026 Edition)", One Useful Thing (Substack), 2026-07-23. https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/an-opinionated-guide-to-which-ai-b22
Re-read in full 2026-10-14 (live page, no archive needed).

## What the lesson uses, checked against the source

Spec uses: C022 point 6 (brief), C103 point 2 (teach, one example), "You vs the agent" (approval mode).

1. **Choose by stakes.** For casual, low-stakes work (recipes, questions, letters) the options, including free default models, "are all at least fine when the stakes are low, so pick the one you like." For high stakes (his examples: a second opinion on a medical or legal matter) use the most advanced models (he names Claude Opus and Fable, GPT-5.6 Sol) set to **at least** "High" thinking; lower error rates, but they cost money. A chart in the post maps model and thinking level to stakes.
2. **Two main choices for real work:** ChatGPT or Claude, starting at $20/month. Other routes (Chinese open-weight models, etc.) are cheaper but need more expertise.
3. **Agent on the company's computer:** ChatGPT Work and Claude Cowork. Quote: "Work and Cowork run on the AI company's computers". So you can start a job from your phone, check later, and schedule recurring tasks; still limited by running on a provided computer. He recommends Sol on High (ChatGPT) and Fable or Opus on High (Claude).
4. **Agent on your own computer:** the desktop apps, ChatGPT's Work and Codex modes and Claude's Cowork and Code modes. Work/Cowork focus on finished results; "Codex and Claude Code expose the work itself": file changes, commands, tests, logs. Example: Codex with GPT-5.6 Sol checked 195 references in a book PDF over about 30 minutes; he found no hallucinations, though it was over-nitpicky. "Computer use" (mouse and browser control) he calls a security concern; proceed carefully.
5. **Connect only what you are comfortable with:** he connects email and "a non-private part of my Google Drive" plus other apps; each person decides their own comfort level. (Used by C103 point 2.)
6. **Approvals:** his seminar test: both agents finished in about 10 minutes; Claude only drafted a reply, ChatGPT sent the email because he had earlier granted it send permission. Rule: "Until you trust the system (and understand its mistakes), leave everything to ask for approval first, which is the default." Keep approval on for anything that "sends, spends, or deletes"; limit what agents can reach.
7. **Prompt injection:** hostile text in emails or web pages can steer an agent; labs are working on it and models are more resistant, "but it is not solved." (Security depth belongs to the security lessons; one line at most here.)
8. **Plans and limits:** the $20 tiers include real but limited agent usage, and agents burn through those limits quickly; more expensive plans "are mostly buying you more hours of AI labor, not smarter AI."
9. **Kind-of-work picks (dated card only):** Research: Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) for analysts and writers. Video: Gemini Omni sees and edits video directly (demo: an 1896 film). Images: ChatGPT and Google have strong built-in generators; Claude has none and draws with code, mixed results. Voice: ChatGPT's GPT-Live listens and speaks natively; Claude's voice is text read aloud. Office documents: Microsoft Copilot "is okay for working with office documents" but lags badly on agentic abilities. Google not recommended as a primary tool now (no leading frontier model, no Codex/Code equivalent), could change.
10. **Cadence:** "Every few months, I write a guide..."; this is the Summer 2026 Edition.

Caveats he states: personal anecdotes; agents sometimes get things wrong or overreach; product naming and docs are confusing; Google's position may change fast.

## What the spec says that the source does NOT say
- "he reissues the guide each season": he says "every few months"; only the title says "Summer 2026 Edition". Say "every few months".
- "for low stakes the chat apps are 'all at least fine'": the "all" refers to the models/options including free defaults, not specifically "chat apps". Close enough, but quote it as "all at least fine when the stakes are low".
- "most advanced model at a High thinking level": source says "at least" High.
- "cloud agents (ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork) run on the company's computers ... while Codex and Claude Code expose the work": Mollick also lists Work and Cowork as modes that run on YOUR computer via the desktop apps; the "expose the work" contrast is drawn inside the own-computer section (Work/Cowork = finished results vs Codex/Code = the work). The spec's framing "cloud agents vs agents that expose the work" merges two separate distinctions (where it runs vs what it shows you). Teach them as two axes.
- Office documents "pick": he does not pick a tool for office documents; he only says Copilot is "okay" for them while lagging as an agent. The card must not present Copilot as his recommendation.
- Nothing in S249 about folders, SSH/WSL, data retention, training settings or memory; those come from other sources.
