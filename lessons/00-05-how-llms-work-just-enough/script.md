In this lesson you'll learn how today's AI models work, and how much that picture has changed in five years: what they take in and make, how they remember, what they cost, and the many places you'll meet them. ## 03 — The desk, five years ago and now
> 2021: GPT-3 via API only, ~2K-token context (2020); ChatGPT came Nov 2022. One-line link to 00.04, no re-teaching.
[[clerk]] The model is a brilliant clerk who knows only what is on the desk right now. [[small-desk]] Five years ago, in autumn twenty twenty-one, the desk was tiny. Text cards only, about two thousand tokens' worth, and one letter slot in the door: an A P I for programmers. No ChatGPT, no memory, no tools. [[now]] Now the desk is the size of a hall, with photos and recordings on it, and rooms, phone lines and a picture studio around it. [[same]] Two things did not change. The clerk still writes one small piece at a time, guessing what comes next. [[overnight]] And nothing stays overnight. Every request starts with a clean desk, and everything is handed over again.

## 04 — Tokens, and a desk that grew
> ~4 characters of English per token (rough). ~2K (2020), 100K Claude (May 2023), 1M Gemini (Feb 2024), 1M standard on Claude's bigger models, not Haiku (card "Oct 2026"). Newer Claude tokenizer ~30% more tokens.
The desk is measured in tokens. [[tokens]] A token is a piece of a word, roughly four characters of English. You're limited and billed in tokens. [[all-counts]] Everything on the desk counts: the app's hidden instructions, every message so far, your files and images, the result of every tool, its thinking, and its reply. [[growth]] The desk grew fast. About two thousand tokens in twenty twenty. A hundred thousand on Claude in twenty twenty-three. A million on Gemini in twenty twenty-four, and today a million is standard on Claude's bigger models. [[meter]] You'll see it as a meter: context used, context left. [[compare]] One catch: token counts don't compare across models. Newer Claude models can count up to a third more tokens for the same text. [[breaks]] Here the picture breaks: no clerk stays. Each reply is a fresh copy that reads the whole desk at once.

## 05 — A fuller desk is a worse desk
> Chroma "context rot", Jul 2025, 18 models; Anthropic docs use the term. "Lost in the middle" (2023). Owner: NEW 03.10 Context engineering (The Machine).
[[pile]] A fuller desk is a worse desk. As it fills, the clerk gets worse, well before it's full. Researchers at Chroma measured it across eighteen models in twenty twenty-five: context rot. [[middle]] Things buried in the middle of a long pile get missed most. [[compact]] So tools now tidy the desk. Compaction summarises the pile onto one page and carries on. Claude Code does it by itself, as auto-compact, or when you type slash compact. [[sweep]] Old tool results get swept off. [[second-clerk]] A sub-agent gets its own fresh desk for a side job, and hands back a short report. [[context-eng]] Prompt engineering has grown into context engineering: choosing what goes on the desk. It gets its own lesson in The Machine.

## 06 — Seeing, hearing, and making
> Claude in: text, images, PDFs; out: text only. Gemini: text, image, audio, video in one request. ChatGPT stitches image and voice models. Sora app shut spring 2026. Owner: NEW 10.14 Multimodal APIs (The Network).
[[inputs]] Five years ago, the desk held only text. Now photos, P D Fs, recordings and video can land on it. That's multimodal, and the word hides three different things. [[reads]] One: the model reads many kinds of input. Claude reads text, images and P D Fs. Gemini takes text, images, audio and video in one request. [[makes]] Two: the model makes pictures or sound itself. That's rarer. [[handoff]] Three, the most common: the app hands the job to a separate specialist model, through an A P I or a tool. The clerk phones the studio next door. [[claude-text]] Claude writes only text. For a chart or a picture, it writes code that draws one, or calls another model through a connector. [[stitched]] ChatGPT feels like one model, but it stitches several together: one for images, one for voice. [[sora]] And OpenAI shut down its Sora video app this spring. You'll meet all this as a paperclip that accepts some files and refuses others. Its own lesson lives in The Network.

## 07 — Diffusion: from static to picture
> Latent diffusion; flow matching; OpenAI image models reported autoregressive (hedged). Diffusion text: Inception's Mercury, Google's experimental diffusion models; no versions or speeds. Owner: NEW 01.13 (The Machine).
Most of those studios run a different kind of model, called diffusion. [[noise]] Picture a page of pure static. [[steps]] The model wipes away a little of the noise, step by step, each step nudged by your prompt, [[picture]] until a picture is left. [[families]] The same trick makes images, video, sound effects and music, as far as their makers have said. Voices mostly aren't. Most current ones use a close cousin called flow matching, which needs fewer steps. [[not-all]] Not every image model is diffusion, though. OpenAI's image models are reported to build pictures piece by piece, like text. [[text-diffusion]] And diffusion can write text, rarely. Inception's Mercury and Google's experimental diffusion models start from a smudged page and sharpen every word at once. Very fast, but not yet frontier quality. [[frontier]] Every top chat model still writes one token after another. How generators work gets its own lesson in The Machine.

## 08 — The knobs, then and now
> Newest Claude models reject non-default temperature / top-p / top-k (no first-version name). Effort picker low · medium · high · xhigh · max. No hallucination rates. Owners: 10.03 settings; 05.08 invented packages.
The lamp over the desk used to have dials. [[temperature]] Temperature: how much randomness goes into each guess. [[top-p]] Top P and top K: how many candidate words the clerk may choose from. [[max]] Max output tokens: a hard cap on the length of the reply. [[taped]] This year, the newest Claude models stopped accepting a custom temperature. Send one, and you get an error. [[effort]] The dial that matters now is effort, or thinking: how long the clerk works before it answers, from low up to max. [[wrong]] And the clerk can still be confidently wrong. Less often with search and tools, but not never. Now it can also take a wrong action, or invent a package that doesn't exist. The settings get their own lesson in The Network.

## 09 — How it "remembers"
> Free Claude memory since 2 Mar 2026; ChatGPT rewrites memories in the background (hedged, no version). Coding agents "mostly" use file search (trend). Spoken "rag" as one word. Owners: NEW 06.07 How AI apps remember; 06.06 RAG (The Code).
The clerk keeps nothing, so every memory is paper put back on the desk. [[five]] There are five ways it comes back. [[convo]] One: the conversation itself. The app re-sends every message, every turn. [[profile]] Two: a memory the assistant keeps about you, a profile it writes itself. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini all have one, and on Claude it's no longer a paid extra. [[search]] Three: searching old chats with a tool. You'll see a line like: searched past chats. [[external]] Four: stores outside the app, reached by tool calls. Memory files on your computer, which Claude Code keeps, or a page in Notion or Google Drive, through a connector. [[rag]] Five: rag, short for retrieval augmented generation, the general name for what three and four do. Fetch the relevant pieces, put them on the desk, then answer. Vector search is one way to fetch; coding agents now mostly search with plain file tools. [[cost]] Each one costs a tool call, desk space, or both. Memory and rag get their own lessons in The Code.

## 10 — Notes pinned to the desk
> Anthropic publishes the Claude apps' system prompts. CLAUDE.md at project root loads at session start; user and company (managed) ones; subfolder ones load when the agent reads there. Claude Code also reads AGENTS.md. Owner: 03.09 (The Machine).
Some paper sits on the desk every turn. [[system]] First, the system prompt: the app's own hidden instructions, read before anything you type. Anthropic publishes the ones for its Claude apps. [[yours]] Second, your standing instructions: preferences, styles and project instructions, set once and re-sent every turn. [[claude-md]] Third, instruction files that load themselves. Put a file called Claude dot M D at the root of a project, and Claude Code reads it at the start of every session there. Your user folder, your company and sub-folders can have one too. [[agents-md]] Agents dot M D is the open version other tools use, and Claude Code now reads it too, when there's no Claude dot M D. Gemini dot M D and Cursor's rules are the same idea. [[not-lock]] But a note is not a lock. Hooks and permissions enforce. A long note gets followed less, and it costs tokens every turn. Instruction files get their own lesson in The Machine.

## 11 — Keeping the desk warm: caching
> Anthropic prompt-caching and Claude Code docs: prefix order tools → system → messages; TTL 5 min default, 1 h optional, hits refresh; Claude Code 1 h on subscription, 5 min on API key. Owner: NEW 10.13 (The Network).
Re-sending the whole desk is slow and expensive, so providers keep it warm. [[layout]] With prompt caching, the provider stores the work it did reading the start of your request, the prefix, and reuses it when the next request starts exactly the same way. [[order]] Order matters: tools first, then the system prompt, then the messages. [[swap]] Change anything near the top, and everything below it is laid out again from scratch. [[timer]] The layout sits under a timer, its time to live. Five minutes by default, or an hour at a higher price, and every reuse resets the clock. Claude Code keeps it an hour on a subscription, and five minutes on an A P I key. [[cold]] Take a longer break, and your next message rebuilds the whole desk. [[breakers]] Other things break it too: switching models, changing tools, dropping old images, compaction, and on older models, effort. Editing Claude dot M D doesn't; the edit waits for your next session. [[not-memory]] The cache isn't the clerk remembering. It's stored arithmetic, thrown away when the timer runs out.

## 12 — The four meters
> Claude API pricing, Oct 2026 (dated card): write 1.25x (5 min) / 2x (1 h); read 0.1x or less; output ~5x input, thinking billed as output. OpenAI and Google cache automatically; Claude API opt-in via cache_control, Claude apps do it for you. Owners: NEW 10.13; 10.11 billing.
That's why the bill has four meters. [[input]] Normal input: fresh paper, full price. [[write]] Cache write: laying the desk out for reuse, at a little more than input. On Claude, a quarter more for five minutes, double for an hour. [[read]] Cache read: reusing the layout, at a tenth of the input price, or less. [[output]] Output: everything the clerk writes, its thinking included. That's the expensive meter, about five times the input price. [[fields]] The A P I reports these four counts. [[long-chat]] So a long chat costs more every turn, even if you type one word, because the whole desk is sent again. It's cheap only while the cache is warm. [[still-fills]] And cached tokens are cheaper, not smaller. They still fill the desk. [[others]] OpenAI and Google cache automatically; on Claude's A P I, you switch it on. The meters get their own lesson in The Network.

## 13 — Rooms and buildings: modes and platforms
> Hedged per plan: merge announced 16 Sep 2026, rolling out to Pro and Max; Team and Free later; Enterprise with notice. Cowork Jan 2026. ChatGPT Chat / Work / Codex desktop (Jul 2026). Gemini CLI folded into Antigravity (Jun 2026). Owners: 03.08, 16.11.
The same clerk works in many rooms and buildings. [[mode-vs-platform]] A mode is the kind of job. A platform is where it runs, and what it can touch. [[modes]] Claude's modes were chat, Cowork and Code. Cowork, launched in January, is an agent for office work on your files. [[merge]] In mid-September, Anthropic began merging chat and Cowork into one Claude that decides whether to answer you or do the work. It's rolling out to Pro and Max first. Claude Code stays its own room. [[buildings]] The buildings: the browser, the desktop app, your phone, the terminal C L I, editor extensions, and cloud sessions that run on Anthropic's machines. [[local-cloud]] Local sees your files, and stops when your laptop sleeps. Cloud sees only what you give it, and keeps going with the lid shut. [[equivalents]] OpenAI pulled Chat, Work and Codex into one desktop app in July. Google folded Gemini C L I into Antigravity in June. Coding agents live in The Machine.

## 14 — Skills, connectors, plugins
> SKILL.md folders, loaded on demand; open standard (Dec 2025), no adopter count spoken. Connectors on MCP. Claude Marketplace (enterprise preview 6 Mar 2026, expanded 23 Sep 2026). Claude Code plugin marketplaces are git repos. OpenClaw skill store, Feb 2026. Owners: 10.10, NEW 10.12, 05.08.
[[skill]] A skill is a folder of instructions: a file called skill dot M D, and sometimes scripts. Only its label sits on the desk; the pages load when the clerk needs them. It's an open format other tools now use too. [[connector]] A connector is a phone line to another service, like Gmail, Notion or GitHub, built on M C P. [[plugin]] A plugin is a boxed kit: skills, connectors, commands and hooks, together. [[shops]] You get them from the app's directory, the Claude Marketplace, reported at over two thousand add-ons, and Claude Code plugin marketplaces, which are git repositories. OpenAI has its own too. [[trust]] Every third-party add-on is code you're trusting. OpenClaw's skill store held hundreds of malicious ones this February. All three live in The Network.

## 15 — Individual vs enterprise
> Consumer training opt-out since Aug to Oct 2025, paid consumer plans included. Team/Enterprise: SSO, admin allow-lists, audit logs, custom retention, managed instruction files, no training by default, advance notice. Since 4 Apr 2026 subscription usage can't power third-party agents (pay-as-you-go extra usage or API key instead). Owners: NEW 15.14, 15.13, 10.11.
Same clerk, different building rules. [[home]] Individual plans, Free, Pro and Max, mostly differ in how much you can use. And since autumn twenty twenty-five, consumer chats, paid ones included, may be used for training unless you turn that off. [[company]] Team and Enterprise add the company building: single sign-on at the door, admins choosing which connectors, plugins and skills are allowed, audit logs, custom data retention, and instruction files pushed to every machine. [[no-training]] Work plans aren't trained on by default, and admins get notice before products change. [[third-party]] Since April, a Claude subscription's included usage can't power third-party agents like OpenClaw. Those are billed separately, by usage or an A P I key. Plans at work get their own lesson in Trust.

## 16 — More rooms: design and voice

Claude voice: bigger models + connectors since Jul 2026, stack undisclosed, never claimed native. GPT-Live full-duplex (Jul 2026); Gemini Live. Owners: NEW 17.10 (Shipping), NEW 10.15 (The Network).
[[design]] Claude Design launched in April as a research preview: describe a visual, and Claude drafts it as layout and code, not with an image model. Since September it lives inside chats too, now in beta, next to Claude Docs and Slides. [[voice]] And voice. It's built two ways. [[pipeline]] A pipeline: speech to text, then the model, then text to speech. It takes turns. [[native]] Or a model that hears and speaks sound directly, catching your tone, and listens while it talks, so you can cut in. OpenAI's G P T Live and Google's Gemini Live work that way. [[claude-voice]] Claude's voice mode can now use the bigger models and your connected apps. It takes turns, and Anthropic hasn't said how it's built. ## 17 — Desks that never close: personal agents
> OpenClaw (Clawdbot Nov 2025, viral Jan 2026); Hermes Agent, Nous Research, Feb 2026, a rival, never "successor". Feb 2026: malicious skills, exposed installs. Meta Muse 8 Sep 2026, US; OpenAI dots 29 Sep 2026, rolling out (lower-case on screen); Gemini Spark, Pro and Ultra. Anthropic: cloud sessions, routines, Dispatch (preview); no forecast. Owners: NEW 16.13, 16.11, 14.12. Hand-over to the guest by name.
Last, a desk that never closes. [[always-on]] A personal agent runs all the time, on its own computer. You message it from a chat app, it keeps a long memory, and it has broad permission to act. [[self-hosted]] One camp is open source and self-hosted. OpenClaw started as Clawdbot, and went viral in January. Hermes Agent, a rival from Nous Research, followed in February, and turns problems it solves into its own skills. Your machine, your keys, your risk. [[security]] In February, OpenClaw had its crisis: more than a hundred thousand installs left open to the internet. [[hosted]] The other camp is hosted by a big company. Meta's Muse launched in September, in the US. OpenAI announced dots at the end of September, rolling out now. Google has Gemini Spark, on its paid AI plans. Their computer, their approval rules. [[anthropic]] Anthropic's nearest pieces are cloud sessions, scheduled routines, and Dispatch, which sends a task from your phone. Their own lesson is in Trust. ## 18 — Myth or still true? Part one [guest]
> Guest: June. Quick-fire myth round: claim, verdict, true version, the literal thing you'll see. Nothing new beyond 04 to 17. Lower-third "guest · June"; score turns livelier.
Thanks. Ten myths, fast. [[myth-1]] Myth one: set temperature to zero, and you get the same answer every time. Wrong twice. The newest Claude models return an error if you set it, and zero never guaranteed the same answer anyway. [[myth-2]] Myth two: it remembers me. Not quite. It re-reads a profile it wrote, searches your old chats, or opens a file. Nothing lives inside the model. [[myth-3]] Myth three: a bigger window means it remembers everything. No. A fuller desk is a worse desk. [[myth-4]] Myth four: all AI art is diffusion. Mostly, not all. Some image models build pictures piece by piece, like text. [[myth-5]] Myth five: Claude can draw that for you. It writes code that draws, or it calls another model. The paintbrush belongs to someone else.
