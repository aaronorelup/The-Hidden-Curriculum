## What the lesson must get right
- **The thesis comes from the evidence, not the original brief.**
  - Opus 5.5 on its own builds a surprising amount: a polished-looking C++ game from one prompt in 89 minutes, and
    recognisable 3D dolls of Jefrie.
  - What "Opus made this" leaves out is what makes the result *yours* and *good*:
    - references and specialised generators, for likeness and voice;
    - a person who looks, listens and plays, because the model can't;
    - direction that moves it off its narrow defaults;
    - the bill, and the pipelines built over months before the "one prompt".
- **Every exhibit carries its caption line from media.json:** maker model, date, time and cost. Claude dollar figures
  are "API-list equivalent"; Aaron pays a subscription. Name the exact model: Opus 5 and Fable 5.1 made the early toy
  Jefries, not Opus 5.5.
- **Say how the experiments were run, briefly and honestly:**
  - each test was one written brief, given word for word;
  - the bare maker had no CLAUDE.md, skills, MCP or web, just an empty folder;
  - technician agents ran and measured each maker without helping it;
  - effort was high throughout;
  - small samples (8 title cards, 6 dusk pieces, 3 seeds per video arm).
- **Where a result contradicts the popular claim, say so plainly:**
  - the C++ game looks finished;
  - five lyric-video versions came out nearly the same picture;
  - the full video pipeline was not better than a good start frame on a simple shot;
  - only 10 of 168 indexed viral "Opus 5.5" videos used an outside video model.
- **The model never played its game, heard its song or watched its clip.** Every bare maker said so in its notes.
  This is a thread through the lesson.
