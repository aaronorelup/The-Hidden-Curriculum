# The hidden curriculum

**Purpose (the first spoken sentence):** In this lesson you'll learn what really stops people from building with AI, why it keeps moving, and how this course keeps up with it.

**Conclusion:** What stops you is small gaps nobody teaches, and they move every few weeks while the model's own knowledge stops at its training date, so this course is a map rebuilt as the gaps move, and your habit is to check the date, the version and the source, and re-test after every update.

**Where it sits on the map:** Start · Orientation — what this is, and your job; module 00 Orientation. This lesson also draws the whole map for the first time (Start, the six districts First Build, Going Live, Directing Well, Agent Power, Running Alone, Beyond Code, then the Capstone), as `curriculum.md` lays it out; the [map] section itself is generated by `tools/course.py sync`.

**Lead source (audit, 2026-10-12):** S033 (Simon Willison); S004 (Aaron Orelup (drafted by Claude from his feedback)). This is the hard-won work the lesson delivers: credit it by name on the title card. The narration names at most the row's sources in curriculum.md (S004, S033, S025, S125, S011); every other source in this spec goes on a dated on-screen card and on the lesson page's source list.

## What to teach (binding scope)

### The premise (frames both concepts; no concept ID of its own)

- **Building is open to anyone now; the ability to judge is not.** Willison dates the turn to November 2025, when coding agents went from "often make mistakes" to "reliable enough to use on a day-to-day basis" (S033). Non-developers now build mainly because it has become accessible, but a 2026 survey of 162 vibe coders found the capacity "to evaluate, debug, and verify remains experience-dependent": access widened, the expertise did not spread with it (S085). Depth: **teach** (two sentences; the evidence for "what stops you" belongs to 00.03).
- **The gap is in the syllabus, not in you.** MIT's Missing Semester exists because tool proficiency is "rarely covered" in classes and "left to students to figure out on their own"; its 2026 edition still teaches the shell, git and shipping next to agentic coding (S209; the quote is on the course's front page, missing.csail.mit.edu, 2026 edition). Aaron's own story (COURSE.md: Python in Notepad saved as .txt, with an IT degree) may carry this, told in the third person. Depth: **brief** (it is the legacy lesson's core and still stands).
- **The topic, not just the facts, goes out of date.** Aaron sent back two of the course's first seven lessons within days: one taught the 2021 picture of a language model, and one, researched with current numbers, still taught hand-debugging, which the agent now does itself. Research made the facts current but could not see that the topic was out of date (S004). That is why this course is built from other people's current, dated work (courses, essays, incident reports, surveys, docs, real questions) and credits each source by name. Depth: **teach** (this is the course's method, said once, plainly).

### C052 What you know goes stale in weeks

1. **Models retire on about 30 days' notice in ChatGPT and Codex.** GPT-5.5 was announced on 2026-09-14 as retiring from ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex on 2026-10-14 (30 days); GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.4 mini were announced on 2026-07-31 as leaving Codex on 2026-08-31; GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark was deprecated the day it was announced, 2026-09-14 (S025). Depth: **teach**. Say it in the past tense with dates: GPT-5.5's retirement is two days after this spec and will be history when the lesson ships.
2. **A change often hits one access route only.** Both retirements above leave the OpenAI API (and API-key Codex sessions for GPT-5.4) untouched: "This retirement does not apply to the OpenAI API" (S025). So know which route you use. Depth: **brief**.
3. **Commands vanish.** `codex mcp-server` was deprecated on 2026-08-24 and removed, with its standalone binary, on 2026-09-05: 12 days (S025). Depth: **teach** (one vivid number).
4. **Products and harnesses move under you.** Codex became part of the ChatGPT desktop app on 2026-07-09 (S025). Claude Code shipped 15 releases between v2.1.282 (2026-09-24) and v2.1.296 (2026-10-09), and a v2.1.288 change (2026-10-02) was reverted in v2.1.289 the next day (S125, entries read 2026-10-12). Depth: **teach** (scroll, don't list).
5. **The best model can vanish or be overtaken.** Claude Fable 5 was shut down by a US export-control directive three days after launch; it had "30 days as definitely the best model, and for 18 of those days it wasn't available", and GPT-5.6 arrived on 9 July, within reach of it; Willison: "you won't get a long time at the top" (S033). Depth: **brief**.
6. **Capabilities change what is worth teaching.** Agents testing their own work in a browser "wasn't true six months ago" (Stanford CS146S, 2026-09-29, S003 line 201), which is part of why hand-debugging left the course (S004). Depth: **brief** (it sets up 00.03).
7. **Updates land on restart.** Claude Code downloads new versions in the background and applies them on the next launch, never mid-session, and a new version typically updates the system prompt or tool definitions; `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1` lets you choose when (S128). So behaviour can change overnight without you doing anything. Depth: **one line**.
8. **Make it a habit.** Read the changelogs of the tools you depend on; re-test your prompts, skills and instruction files after a model or harness update; treat any how-to older than a few months as suspect; keep prompts and checks usable across models (S004, S025, S033). Depth: **teach** (this is the takeaway).
- Dropped here: **hard-coded model names break silently** in configs, scripts and scheduled tasks (S025's "Update workspace defaults, saved model settings, managed configurations, custom agents, scheduled tasks"). One sentence at most; the silent-failure side is taught in 08.02 green-is-not-success and the scheduled-agent side in 08.01.

### C024 Generated is not looked up: the model's knowledge has a date

1. **Without a search or retrieval tool, an answer is generated from the weights, not fetched.** Lin (PNAS Nexus, July 2026) cites research showing users "conflate generation with search" (S011). Depth: **teach**.
2. **The weights stop at a training date and change only when the model is retrained** (S011: "stateless" means the weights are fixed during inference). Everything in points 1-5 of C052 happened after that date for any model trained before it. Depth: **teach**. A concrete, current number (every current Claude model lists a reliable knowledge cutoff of June 2026) comes from a page not yet in sources.md: see the candidate below, and add it before scripting.
3. **Agents confidently write for things that changed after training.** MCP's specification revision of 2026-07-28 retired parts of the 2025 protocol that most explainers and training data still describe; tell the agent which revision to target (S059). A model ID remembered from training may already be retired (S025). Depth: **brief** (one example on screen).
4. **Tools bring in current facts.** Retrieval raised GPT-4's FreshQA accuracy by 32.6-49.0% relative to the same model without it (S011); give the agent current documentation instead of its memory, for example through a docs MCP server such as Context7 (S003 line 107), and stale docs are worse than none (S003 line 145). Depth: **brief**.
5. **So check three things on anything time-sensitive:** the date, the version, the source; and look in the transcript for an actual search or fetch call before believing "I looked it up" (S011, S025). Depth: **teach**.
- Dropped here: web search pulling "most available, not most reliable" sources and a helper model summarising pages the answer then cites (C024 points 4-5, S010) → 09.01 checking-research-numbers-and-parts. Survey figures on how people judge AI answers (S080) → 00.03.

### Course mechanics (one line each, no concept ID)

- **The map is rebuilt as the gaps move.** Every lesson has a permanent key, the order and numbers change when the map changes, and legacy lessons stay published until rebuilt (`curriculum.md`, `docs/course-data.md`). Depth: **one line**.
- **The people who use it feed it.** Anyone can open an issue on the course's GitHub repo for a gap they wish they had known, a question, a source, or something wrong or out of date; each becomes evidence for a concept (`curriculum.md` intro). Depth: **one line**. As of 2026-10-12 the repo exists but is empty (checked with `gh api`), so the issue chooser must be live before this is shown or narrated as working.
- **The rule, named once.** The agent handles what can be verified; you check what it cannot, or bring in an expert. One sentence only; 00.03 teaches it.

## You vs the agent

As of 2026-10-12:
- **The agent** can search, fetch current documentation, cite URLs with dates and versions when it has the tools (S003, S011), read changelogs, search a project for pinned model IDs and update them, and run a scheduled check (S025).
- **The agent cannot** know what changed after its training date unless it reads current sources, will not plan for its own model disappearing (S033, S004), and cannot tell when a whole topic is out of date: Aaron's research step made facts current and still missed that (S004).
- **You** know which tools, models and access routes (app sign-in or API key) you depend on (S025); decide when to update (S128); ask for sources with dates on anything time-sensitive and open at least one; check the transcript for a real search call (S011); re-test your prompts, skills and checks after an update (S004, S025); and send the course the gaps you hit.
- **Bring in an expert** when you cannot judge whether a dated, cited source is right (medical, legal, financial claims): taught in 09.01, and how to bring one in, in 04.04.

## Out-of-date framings to avoid

- "Learn one model and one tool and you're set"; "Pick the best model once and build on it" (S025, S033).
- "A tutorial from last year still applies" (S004).
- Change measured in years ("new models every few months"): the legacy 00.03 scale. In 2026 the unit is days and weeks (S025, S125, S033).
- "Codex is a separate app" (S025, since 2026-07-09).
- "Set the model once in config and forget it" (S025).
- "The AI looked it up" for an answer with no tool call; "if it sounds specific, it's current" (S011).
- 2025-era MCP explainers: stateful sessions, SSE transport, server callbacks over an open stream (S059).
- "Models retire on 30 days' notice" with no qualifier: that is OpenAI's pattern for ChatGPT and Codex sign-in, not the API, and Anthropic's published API retirement dates are a year or more out (S025; candidate models page). Say which vendor and which route.
- The six legacy districts (The Machine, The Code, The History, The Network, Trust, Shipping) or the five-stop road as the course map: both retired on 2026-10-12 (`curriculum.md` legacy table).
- "Data Science" in Aaron's degree: the legacy 00.01 fact-check found only "Information Systems" on the record; COURSE.md still says both. Use "Information Systems" unless Aaron confirms.
